WellKept Feature Design

Insight Engine

A home that thinks about itself, even when the homeowner isn't. Proactive, personalized insights surfaced on the Today screen — from data gaps to seasonal checklists.

Philosophy
Design Principles
Six guiding principles that shape every decision in the insight engine.
Principle 01

Freshness Over Volume

One insight at a time, but always something new when the user returns. Weekly cadence, not daily or per-visit.

Principle 02

Earn the Right to Advise

Early on, focus on collecting data (completeness coach). As the profile fills in, transition to proactive advisor.

Principle 03

Never Feel Like Homework

The app should feel alive and helpful, not like a checklist nagging you. Respect the user's time and knowledge level.

Principle 04

No Filler

If we genuinely have nothing new, show a positive "all clear" state. Bad content erodes trust faster than silence.

Principle 05

Accept Fuzzy Data

"About 6 years old" is plenty. Don't require serial numbers to deliver value.

Principle 06

Immediate Payoff

When a user invests time answering questions, the next insight must demonstrate value from what they just shared. Data in → value out, every time.

Onboarding
First-Time Experience & Payoff Loop
The first insight is critical. The system earns trust through an immediate data-in → value-out feedback loop.

1. Initial Insight (with Rentcast data)

Acknowledge what we already know, then ask about the most impactful gap.

"We know your home was built in 1998 with central air. Tell us a bit about your major systems — age, last service, anything you know."

2. Initial Insight (no enrichment)

Warm onboarding prompt. Low-pressure, signals a small time investment.

"Let's get to know your home. A few quick questions about your biggest systems will help us keep everything on track."

3. Payoff — Immediate Value

After the first data gap is completed, the dirty flag triggers re-evaluation. The very next insight references what the user just told us.

"Now that we know your HVAC is 9 years old, here's what that means: you've got about 5-6 years left on a typical lifespan. No action needed yet, but worth budgeting ahead."

4. Loop Reinforcement

This payoff loop is mandatory for the first 3–4 interactions. The evaluator prioritizes "first proactive insight about recently-provided data" above normal ranking until the user has experienced the full cycle at least twice.

Design Decisions
Key Decisions & Tradeoffs
The reasoning behind the most consequential architectural and UX choices.
Why one card at a time?
Signal-to-noise. Multiple cards create paralysis. One card says "this is the most important thing right now." The user builds a habit: open app, see card, take action or dismiss, done. Each card earns screen time by being the best candidate.
Why weekly batch, not real-time?
LLM cost control + predictability. Batch generation means we call the LLM once per home per week, generating 5-7 insights as buffer. Event-triggered regeneration uses a dirty flag, debounced to 1 hour. Only truly urgent items bypass the batch.
Why no LLM in the evaluator?
Determinism + speed. The evaluator runs pure logic over the database. Priority ranking must be predictable and auditable. The LLM only enters at content generation — deciding what to say is deterministic, how to say it is creative.
Why accept fuzzy data (30% weight on "acknowledged + rough age")?
Activation energy. If completing a system requires precise data (serial number, install date, model), users won't do it. "I have central AC, about 6 years old" reaches ~55% score on that system. 50-60% should feel like meaningful progress, not failure.
Why a three-layer quality gate?
Trust. An insight that mentions equipment you don't have, repeats last week's card, or shows a 500-character wall of text erodes trust instantly. The structural, relevance, and dedup checks catch LLM hallucinations and repetition before they reach the user.
Why "earned silence" for all-clear?
No filler principle. Showing a generic tip because the queue is empty teaches the user to ignore the card slot. A warm "all clear" state is positive reinforcement — it means the insight engine looked and found nothing to flag. That's valuable information.
Why template + LLM generator (not all-LLM)?
Cost + reliability. Warranty expiry warnings and completeness prompts don't need an LLM. Templates handle ~40% of insight types at zero LLM cost. LLM mode is reserved for spending narratives, lifespan context, and seasonal advice — content that benefits from natural language fluency.
Why tight "urgent" threshold?
Boy-who-cried-wolf risk. Only safety risks, imminent warranty expiry (<30d on >$1K equipment), and critical system maintenance overdue by 2x qualify as urgent. Everything else that feels "important" is P3 (proactive). If urgent is overused, users stop paying attention.
UX Flows
Interaction Patterns
Four primary flows from insight card to action completion.
Data Gap → AI Chat

"Tell us about your electrical system"

  1. User sees QUICK Q card on Today
  2. Taps "Let's do it"
  3. AI chat opens, pre-seeded with system context
  4. AI asks 2-3 focused questions, logs equipment
  5. Completeness updates; next insight references new data
Seasonal → Checklist

"Summer's coming — prep your home"

  1. User sees SEASONAL card
  2. Taps "Create my summer checklist"
  3. System generates todos from climate zone + systems
  4. Dedup against existing recurring services & todos
  5. Todos created with staggered due dates
Proactive → Advisory

"Your HVAC is entering its second decade"

  1. User sees INSIGHT card
  2. Taps "Tell me more"
  3. AI chat opens with lifespan context
  4. Agent advises on watch-for signs, budgeting, quotes
  5. May offer to create a task or log details
Urgent → Task

"Smoke detector batteries are 2+ years old"

  1. User sees HEADS UP card
  2. Taps "Add to my tasks"
  3. Todo created immediately
  4. Or "Remind me later" snoozes for 7 days
Runtime
Scheduling & Triggers
Two trigger modes keep the queue fresh without over-spending on LLM calls.

Weekly Batch (Primary)

Runs once per week per home
Evaluator → Generator → 5-7 insights queued
Clears expired insights from previous batch
Startup catch-up for missed windows
Runs as setInterval in the Express process

Event-Triggered (Supplemental)

Significant data changes set dirty flag
Service logged, equipment added, questions answered
Next batch picks up dirty homes first
1-hour debounce: multiple changes = one flag
Exception: urgent safety items bypass batch
